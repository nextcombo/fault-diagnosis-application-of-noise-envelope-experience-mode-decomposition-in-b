# [Fault Diagnosis] Application of Noise Envelope Experience Mode Decomposition in Bearing Fault Diagnosis [with MATLAB Code]

The objective noise-assisted decomposition method, namely ensemble empirical mode decomposition (EEMD) and complementary EEMD (CEEMD), can overcome the shortcomings of EMD but cannot completely eliminate the presence of white noise. This paper proposes an improved noise-eliminated EEMD (nease-eliminated EEMD) method to further reduce the white noise within the internal function and maintain optimal integration. The NEEEMD algorithm also uses EMD to decompose white noise signals and subtracts from the output of EEMD. The performance of NEEEMD is validated using simulated signals through root mean square error (RRMSE) and time-envelope spectral peakiness (TESK). A sensitive mode selection method based on the product of time domain kurtosis and frequency domain energy entropy is proposed for selecting the most sensitive intrinsic mode functions from NEEEMD. Finally, to enhance the pulses related to faults in the signal, a high-level filter called MOMEDA is applied to the most sensitive IMF. Results show the effectiveness of this method by analyzing the envelop spectrum of bearing signals containing different types of faults under different rotational speeds and loads. Envelop spectrum analysis indicates that the proposed method can provide more fault-related pulses in each case, demonstrating better performance. Conclusion: The method proposed in this paper can identify more fault-related pulses from the envelop spectrum, making it a more accurate rotor bearing fault diagnosis system.
Introduction
Rolling bearings and other rotating machinery are among the key components in various industries. Bearings and other rotating parts suffer continuous failure due to their long-term operation. Having a reliable fault diagnosis and health monitoring system is essential to avoid any unnecessary failures during operation. Over the past few decades, both industry and academia have conducted extensive research to ensure safe operation [1].
In the research on bearing fault diagnosis and condition monitoring, signal processing has always been the most prominent and successful method [2-4]. The most effective signal processing techniques include time-frequency domain analysis, including wavelet transforms, variational mode decomposition (VMD), local mean decomposition (LMD), and EMD, which have been applied in bearing fault diagnosis. The EMD series has proven to be superior because it acts as a filter by generating several IMFs [11].
Empirical Mode Decomposition (EMD) is a popular time-frequency adaptive decomposition method suitable for the analysis of changing and non-stationary signals. EMD has been widely used in fault diagnosis and condition monitoring [12-14]. EMD decomposes a signal into several components called Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs) and a monotonic signal called residual. Xiong et al. [16] obtained acceleration time-series from a bearing housing and processed it using the EMD method with a hybrid peak kurtosis criterion to remove noise components that exhibit signal trends. Simulation results confirmed the effectiveness and practicality of the proposed method. Song et al. [17] proposed a new multilayer filtering method to remove noise and obtain weak fault features from different layers obtained using EMD. Simulation and experimental results showed that this method can effectively eliminate noise and improve image recognition accuracy.
However, one of the main limitations of EMD is the mode mixing problem, which is described as blending elements from different scales into a single IMF or separate IMFs. Due to this drawback, the signal may contain components and noise that are unrelated to the signal, thereby reducing system accuracy. To overcome the limitations of EMD, Wu and Huang [18] proposed EEMD, an enhanced version of EMD with noise assistance. EEMD incorporates white noise into the signal, filling it throughout the entire signal, thus completely resolving the mode mixing issue. Zuo [19] applied deep neural networks to the processing signals of EEMD. This method significantly improves the accuracy of fault diagnosis in small sample cases. Li [20] introduced a new method for selecting sensitive IMFs shared with high-frequency band entropy for bearing fault diagnosis. The new method outperforms other methods in feature extraction. However, to significantly reduce the presence of white noise, many sets are needed, which increases computational complexity.
To alleviate the shortcomings of EEMD, Yeh et al. [21] proposed the CEEMD method, which adds positive and negative white noise in pairs to reduce residual noise in the reconstructed signal. Yang [22] used wavelet thresholding combined with CEEMD to reduce background noise from vibration signals from rolling bearings. Using CEEMD to obtain IMFs, and selecting them based on kurtosis index and correlation coefficient. Employing envelope spectrum to extract fault impulses. Lu [23] used an optimized CEEMD assisted system combined with a rigid Gaussian distribution clustering unsupervised learning algorithm for bearing fault diagnosis. The model has self-learning capabilities. Although CEEMD can largely reduce white noise, it does not entirely eliminate its limitations, necessitating further research. This paper proposes a new EMD method that overcomes the shortcomings of EMD and CEEMD by subtracting noise integrated IMFs from the integrated IMFs of the original signal. NEEEMD reduces the shortcomings of EMD and CEEMD, validated using RRMSE and TESK in limited ensembles.
Due to the decomposition algorithm generating multiple IMFs, and not all IMFs having the same physical significance, it is important to identify which IMFs further work. Typically, higher-order IMFs contain the most information about the signal [24]. Therefore, selecting the most significant SMs from IMFs becomes crucial. Among many techniques proposed for SM selection, He et al. [25] introduced a method of selecting SMs through variance regression. Yan and Gao [26] used correlation measures and energy quantities to select the most significant SMs. Lei and Zuo [27] proposed measuring the correlation between vibration signals with fault states and normal state IMFs for SM selection. Wang et al. [27] suggested selecting the IMF with the largest kurtosis value as the most significant SM. However, all the methods listed above were derived from the time-domain analysis of the acquired IMFs, overlooking the time-frequency domain characteristics of SMs. This paper proposes a sensitive pattern selection method that considers both time-domain kurtosis and frequency-domain energy entropy in extracting the most information from the signal.
The noise-assisted decomposition method can decompose the signal into individual modes, where the most sensitive mode contains the highest information. However, feature faulty impulses may be very low because noise occupies the entire frequency bandwidth and white noise exists in each mode. Therefore, in rotating machinery fault diagnosis, it is necessary to enhance the faulty pulse of the fault frequency. The minimum entropy deconvolution (MED) [28] has been effectively used by many researchers for rotating machinery fault diagnosis; however, this method also has its own limitations. MED requires multiple iterations to solve, and most importantly, it is suitable for single pulses, whereas our signals have multiple pulses. McDonald and Zhao [29] proposed a powerful method called Multi-Point Optimal Minimum Entropy Back Filtering Adjustment (MOMEDA), which does not require any iteration to obtain the filtering solution. MOMEDA can utilize multi-point peaks within the periodic range to extract regular fault impacts. Since it does not need to predetermine the fault cycle, it has strong generality. Zhu et al. [30] utilized MOMEDA to extract effective components from bearing signals, then applied the Teager Energy operator (TEO) to obtain fault features. Wang et al. [31] applied MOMEDA to both simulated signals and gearbox vibration signals, effectively separating various influencing factors. This paper applies MOMEDA to the most sensitive mode after NEEEMD decomposition of IMFs. The length of the filter varies within a range to obtain the best output with the maximum faulty pulse from the envelope spectrum. Different bearing fault situations were considered under different rotational speeds and motor loads to summarize the adaptability of the proposed method.
Comparative analysis of faulty pulses from the envelope spectrum indicates the superiority of the system, individually compared to EEEMD and CEEMD.

Conclusion
Compared with existing EEMD and CEEMD methods, this paper proposes a time-frequency adaptive NEEEMD method to address the white noise issue in EEMD. The results show that the proposed NEEEMD method based on RRMSE and TESK has better robustness. In all cases, the performance of the proposed method is superior to other methods, which proves that for the RRMSE results, our method contains less white noise, and for the TESK results, our method provides the most useful information. On this basis, a signal intensity estimation method based on time domain kurtosis and frequency domain energy entropy is proposed. This method multiplies the time domain kurtosis and frequency domain energy entropy to obtain the signal intensity that considers both time and frequency domains. Additionally, to enhance the impulse response of fault frequencies, SM uses a filter called MOMEDA. To verify the effectiveness of this method, it is applied to a bearing dataset. The envelope spectrum of maximum SM is plotted separately for EEMD, CEEMD, and NEEEMD + MOMEDA fault pulse diagrams. Our proposed method (NEEEMD + MOMEDA) has more visual features of fault pulses in all cases, thereby outperforming existing methods. From this study, we can conclude:
(1) Compared with EEMD and its improved CEEMD, NEEEMD more effectively reduces the presence of white noise.
(2) The proposed SM selection method is superior to most other methods used by previous researchers because it considers both time and frequency domains.
(3) The MOMEDA filter combined with the most sensitive IMF in NEEEMD can effectively obtain more fault-related pulses.
